Frequently asked questions

How do I turn on ScriptBlocker?

Mac: open Safari, choose Safari › Settings › Extensions, select ScriptBlocker, and turn it on. Then allow it on all websites.

iPhone and iPad: open Settings › Apps › Safari › Extensions, tap ScriptBlocker, turn on Allow Extension, and under Permissions set All Websites to Allow.

The ScriptBlocker app shows whether the extension is on and has a button that takes you to these settings.

Where do I find the switch?
On Mac, click the ScriptBlocker button in Safari's toolbar. On iPhone and iPad, open the page menu in Safari's address bar and tap ScriptBlocker. The toolbar button shows a switch that is on where JavaScript runs and off where it's blocked.
Why does Safari say ScriptBlocker can read sensitive information on websites?
Safari shows this warning for any extension that works on all websites. ScriptBlocker needs that access to block scripts on the sites you choose and to show the site you're on. It doesn't read or collect the contents of pages, and it never sends anything off your device. See the Privacy Policy for details.
A website looks broken or empty. What do I do?
Many websites need JavaScript to work. Open ScriptBlocker on that site and turn its switch back on. The page reloads with JavaScript allowed. If you've blocked JavaScript on every website, turn the JavaScript switch back on first.
Why does the page reload when I flip a switch?
Blocking takes effect when a page loads, so ScriptBlocker reloads the current page whenever its setting changes.
Does blocking a website also block its subdomains?
Yes. Blocking example.com also blocks news.example.com and any other subdomain. On a blocked website, ScriptBlocker blocks every script the page loads, including scripts from other websites, and the scripts written into the page itself.
What happens to my list when I block JavaScript everywhere?
Your list is kept. While JavaScript is off for every website, the list has no effect. When you turn JavaScript back on, the websites on your list stay blocked.
Do my blocked websites sync between my devices?
No. Each Mac, iPhone, and iPad keeps its own list, stored by Safari on that device.
Does ScriptBlocker work in Private Browsing?
Yes, if you allow it. On Mac, select ScriptBlocker in Safari › Settings › Extensions and turn on Allow in Private Browsing. On iPhone and iPad, turn on Allow in Private Browsing in Settings › Apps › Safari › Extensions › ScriptBlocker.
Is ScriptBlocker an ad blocker?
No. It blocks JavaScript, nothing else. Ads and trackers that rely on scripts usually disappear on blocked websites, but images, styles, and other content still load.

Known limitations

  • Websites that are built entirely with JavaScript won't work while JavaScript is blocked.
  • Your list of blocked websites isn't synced between devices.
  • ScriptBlocker only affects Safari. Other browsers, and web views inside other apps, aren't affected.
